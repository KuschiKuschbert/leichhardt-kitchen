# Leichhardt Kitchen Book

The Leichhardt Hotel kitchen's app: the recipe book, the prep list, the order list, the wastage log, the transfer list (stock shared with the other venues) and the team's how-to guides, plus the head chef's admin panel. It works on any phone, tablet or computer with a web browser and can be installed as an app. Working rules for changing it are in `CLAUDE.md`.

## What it does

- **Individual shift pings (26 Oct 2026).** Write a ping has a Send to dropdown: Everyone, or one active kitchen person. Individual messages are private to that person and the chefs who manage pings. Their existing schedule, 30-minute window and Got it tracking stay. Recipients stay fixed after saving; Use again makes a copy for someone else. Old apps keep Everyone pings and need a refresh for individual pings. Both kinds are included in backups. See [individual pings](docs/ping-recipients.md).

- **Warm button feedback (25 Oct 2026).** The chef hat and phone navigation icons tilt slightly left on hover or while pressed. Navigation labels stay still. Buttons and chips lift a little under a mouse, press down and release softly; menu rows gain a warm tint and their icons nudge across and tilt left, tilting a little further while pressed. The chef menu opens with a short fade and settles into place. Its hat button stays theme orange with the hat tilted left while the dropdown is open, then returns to normal when it closes. Touch screens use press feedback without a lingering hover. Keyboard focus stays visible, disabled controls stay still, and reduced motion removes the movement while keeping colour feedback. A small pointer handler keeps navigation press feedback visible on touch screens and clears it on release or cancellation. No extra reads or saved data.

- **Compact ways back (24 Oct 2026, approved Header arrow design).** One 20 px arrow in the existing header replaces the book mark on task pages, inside a transparent 44 px tap area. No extra Back row, editor return footer or floating recipe return button. Its accessible label and tooltip name the destination. Supplier review and catalogue editing show one task heading; the supplier workspace returns with its draft intact. Local tasks step back first, unsaved warnings appear separately, and pending saves block return. Direct links have a safe local parent. Settings Done and phone Back retain their existing behaviour. Parent returns reuse the existing parent history entry, or replace a direct-link entry when its parent was not visited (25 Oct 2026). Repeated Back keeps moving out of the task instead of reopening the editor. See `docs/navigation.md`.
- **Two ways to look at the recipes.** The Pass opens first. The Pass / Tiles switch next to the search box changes it any time (and **Settings** sets which one opens):
  - **Tiles:** every recipe as a card with its small drawn dish, with search, recipe type and "Free from" filters on top.
  - **The Pass:** dishes drawn as plates and batch recipes as GN pans. Tap a plate to zoom in, then tap the laksa on the barramundi to open the laksa. The allergen lens shows where an allergen is at every level.
- **Linked batch recipes.** Any ingredient that has its own recipe is underlined orange with a "Recipe 06" tag, so tapping it opens that recipe. Batch recipes show which dishes they go on. A dish used on another dish shows it too ("Goes on": Side Salad on the Grill Maiden's Board), and a dish's tip box shows on the Pass as well as in Tiles (23 Oct 2026).
- **Allergens roll up.** A dish picks up the allergens of the batch recipes it uses, and names where each one comes from. A "?" means "may be in it, check the supplier label".
- **Cards to come.** A batch recipe that isn't in the book yet shows as "Card to come". Every dish that uses one is marked "Allergens incomplete", is left out of the Free from filter, and counts as "may have" under the allergen lens, until that card is added.
- **Set out like the guest menu.** Recipes are grouped in the order the menu prints them: Snackin' Plates, Salads, Big Country Feeds, Off the Grill, Toppers, Schnittys & Parmys, Hand Held, Sides, Dessert, Seniors and Kids, then the Functions platters. Tiles and The Pass both follow it, and each section is also a filter chip in Tiles. **Specials** (27 Oct 2026) always comes first: its shelf tops the Pass and Tiles, and its orange chip sits first after All and shows only the specials. It shows only while a dish is in it; the head chef's Special switch in the recipe form puts a dish there (no rules change: it is a section of the guest menu order in `book/index`). The Functions chip is a rounded rectangle and sits next. Batch recipes and components come after the menu sections ("Bains and prep" on The Pass). On the Pass the Batch recipes chip shows only the batch recipes on that shelf, and Components only the components (23 Oct 2026; both showed all 46).
- **From the side.** Plated dishes also show a labelled side view, so the team can see what sits on top. Burgers show their build, top to bottom, instead.
- **Pin labels.** On the plate drawing the page places each label where it does not cover the food, other labels or other pins, with a thin line back to its pin. It reads the drawing to find the food, so a new dish needs no label tuning. Boards and platters (more than 8 pins) use smaller labels. The ring is see-through so the food shows through it.
- **Manage Recipes & Drawings (head chef only, 21 Oct 2026).** The head chef changes any recipe or adds a new one in the book (chef hat menu, or Change this recipe on a card). Staff phones show the change within half an hour, or straight away when the book opens. See Manage Recipes & Drawings below.
- **Functions (21 Oct 2026).** A function manager books a function once (chef hat, Functions): the date, event start, guests, platters from the book's Functions section, and when they go out. The kitchen sees it in a band under the top bar on every page on the day, with the next round, and taps it for the full timetable and the kitchen comment. See Functions below.
- **Chef to-do (23 Oct 2026).** The head chef and the sous chefs share one to-do list (chef hat, Chef to-do): add a job with a day it is due by and who it is for, tick it off, change or remove it. Staff never see it.
- **Tick off steps** while cooking. The ticks clear when the page is closed.
- **Search (October 2026):**
  - Every word counts, in any order: "parmy chicken" finds Chicken Parmy.
  - "Gluten free", "gf", "no dairy", "df", "nut free", "egg free", "shellfish free" and so on show the recipes free from it, by the same rule as the Free from chips (no "may have", and no recipe with a card still to come). A note above the results says so. It also works with a word: "gf burger". So the Pass, which has the allergen lens instead of Free from chips, can do free-from too.
  - A word that is one slip away from a word in the book is put right when nothing else matches ("chiken" shows chicken, with a note saying so). Searches that still find nothing are queued for the admin panel as before.
  - The search bar stays at the top of the screen while the list scrolls. On phones the "16 of 114 match" count shows while a search or filter is on. Switching between the Pass and Tiles starts at the top of the list.
- **Recipe cards on phones and tablets:** the drawings come first, so a row under the name trail jumps straight to Allergens, Ingredients or Method. It is hidden on wide screens, where the card is in two columns.
- **Settings** (the chef hat at the top right, then Settings):
  - Which view it opens with: The Pass and Tiles each show a small drawing of the recipes page on a computer and on a phone (`miniShot()` in `js/app.js`), so staff see the layout before they pick. The one in use says "Opens with this".
  - Text size (Normal, Large, Extra large)
  - Head chef only (shows only on a device where the head chef is signed in, see Admin has one home): Clear ticked steps, and Shared kitchen device (Personal phone or Shared device): on a shared device the prep pages ask who you are again after 10 minutes idle and each new day; see Shared kitchen device under Staff lock. The choice stays on the device after the head chef signs out.
  - Open on a phone (computers and wide tablets only, 900 px and wider): the QR code (`qr.svg`) that opens the book, with Print the A4 sign (`qr-sign.pdf`) and Download the QR code (`qr.png`)

  Settings are saved on each phone or tablet.

## Supplier order and catalogue (24 Oct 2026)

**Chef hat > Supplier order** (`#/suppliers`) is for the head chef, sous chefs and the verified Google owner. Staff, leads and function managers cannot open it or read its data. It is lazy-loaded, uses the current Kitchen Book fonts and controls, and has a collapsible supplier sidebar. The selected supplier shows current pars, exact source names, pack details and quantities from its last recorded order, with weekday and time in Queensland.

**Catalogue:** Admin > Content > Suppliers & pars opens the same editor as Manage catalogue in Supplier order. Only the head chef (their own PIN) or the verified owner edits it. The full admin panel still needs Google. Edit current pars, add manual items, keep source records, set units and verified pack conversions, add confirmed matching names, archive or restore. Blank pars and zero are different. Saved changes have a revision and audit entry; a stale version never silently overwrites another device. Imported product names and original pars stay unchanged. Discontinued items are archived, not deleted.

**Private import:** the editor accepts a checked version-1 JSON catalogue, previews its suppliers and item count, and asks which existing reminder each supplier belongs to. Supplier goods and source documents are never public assets. The private import file is supplied separately. New catalogue items are appended; reimporting preserves edited pars and manual additions. No-op imports do not create revisions. If an import stops part way, the page names how many suppliers were saved and keeps the remaining plan.

**Orders:** enter a number, review, then Record order placed after placing the actual order with the supplier. A number uses the configured order unit when one is shown. With no configured unit it still saves, shown as e.g. 2 (unit not set); explicit entries such as 2 kg continue to work. Stock counts also accept numbers, including zero. Unit-dependent top-ups stay off until units and any conversion are confirmed. Catalogue changes preserve the meaning of existing draft quantities, and old history never gains a unit retroactively. This sends nothing to a supplier. An immutable snapshot keeps names, quantities, packs, pars, source, writer and time. The same transaction saves its linked cut-off occurrence and the existing Ordered tick. A retry keeps its order ID; another chef's order asks for an explicit Additional order. Copy and Use quantities only fill a draft. An item absent from the last supplier order says Not ordered. History is loaded in pages, only when asked.

**Existing reminders:** a supplier name on the cut-off bar or tick list opens its sheet; phone pushes include a validated local supplier/cut-off link. A cut-off with no catalogue supplier, or more than one, opens a short card instead (27 Oct 2026): "Valley Veg is not in the supplier catalogue", when it closes, the bar's own **Order done** (today's and tomorrow's cut-offs, with the same Undo) and the Order list. It never opens another supplier's sheet and reads no order history. The head chef sees where to link it (Supplier settings); Refresh opens the sheet once one supplier is linked. **Order done** on the reminder bar confirms an order placed elsewhere without asking for quantities or units, including for linked suppliers. It saves only the existing shared completion tick, writer and time. The saved confirmation offers Undo; unticking on the Order list also reopens the reminder without deleting history. No catalogue read is needed, no empty order is invented, and detailed drafts, stock counts and previous quantities stay intact. The tick list's linked-supplier action still opens detailed review. A failed completion restores the reminder; a queued offline save is not announced as confirmed before the server accepts it. The staff free-text Order list and its item ticks continue as before.

**Suggestions:** show why, then Apply. Repeat previous quantities, use a fresh counted-stock top-up with confirmed units/conversion, or flag an exact product name/confirmed alias from the staff Order list. Ambiguous requests stay unmatched; supplier orders do not tick staff requests automatically. No guessed consumption, automatic orders or automatic par changes. Suggestions never overwrite typed quantities.

**Signal, privacy and saved work:** private catalogue/history use Firestore Lite on the existing Firebase app, without a persistent catalogue cache. They need a connection. Drafts are held in memory for the current signed-in session; signing out or changing the person clears them. Save errors retain inputs. A timed-out save is unconfirmed: check the latest history or catalogue before retrying. No signal or a refused read is never treated as an empty catalogue. Missing module, mismatched API and unpublished rules have retry/access messages. Other kitchen pages retain their current offline behaviour.

**Reads and data:** catalogue is one query on entry or Refresh (one read per supplier); latest order is one read on supplier opening; history/audit are bounded pages, no per-item history reads or polling. Records are `suppliers/<id>`, its `orders` and `audit` subcollections, and `supplierlinks/<cut-off day>_<cut-off id>`. Existing `cutdone` keeps its old shape. The manual backup, nightly backup and PIN migration include the new records through the private client. Rules must be published before the client code.

`js/supplier-core.js` contains the shared validation, quantity and suggestion maths; `js/supplier-store.js` the online-only client and transactions; `js/suppliers.js` the interface. None belongs in the app's start-up shell. `docs/supplier-workspace.md` is the accepted contract and verification record.

A pre-existing fallback-font problem found by the baseline tests was corrected at the same time: wide top-bar navigation may wrap so Kitchen Book remains on one line at 1280 px when the web font has not loaded.

## Delivery temperatures (26 Oct 2026)

Open **Delivery received** in the chef-hat dropdown. Enter the supplier, check the Queensland date and time, and enter a chiller / coldroom reading, a freezer reading, or both. Zero, negative readings and decimals are accepted. Add items or notes if needed, then **Save delivery**. The saved receipt includes who received it. Everyone with a personal login can use it, including Functions and View only. Signed-out visitors cannot open delivery records.

Today's receipts appear below the form. The verified Google admin can select an earlier date and record an earlier arrival. Saved readings stay as recorded; add a new receipt with an explanatory note for a correction. Failed saves keep their details, and retry checks the same id to prevent duplicate receipts. Delivery data and drafts are cleared on sign-out or account change. The form shares the book's Barlow fonts, colours and controls. The longer chef-hat menu scrolls within 60% of the viewport while preserving 48 px tap targets.

`js/deliveries.js` loads only for `#/deliveries`. Today's page uses one live filtered query, removed on exit; admin history uses one date query on selection or Refresh. No delivery reads on normal startup and no persistent private cache. The database enforces login, today's staff access, input shape and immutable readings. The existing 400-day manual and 45-day nightly backup windows include receipts. PIN moves carry all receipts. See `docs/delivery-received.md` for the data contract and release evidence.

## Adding or changing a recipe

All recipes are in **`js/data.js`**. The top of that file explains every field. Recipes the head chef changes or adds in the book itself are kept in Firestore and laid over this file, and the book's copy wins (see Recipes changed in the book below). To add one here:

1. Copy an existing recipe block, from `{` to `},`.
2. Give it a new `id` (short, no spaces, for example `gravy`) and the next card number.
3. Fill in the name, yield, ingredients, steps and allergens.
4. To make an ingredient tappable, add `link: 'gravy'` (the other recipe's `id`).
5. Put anything that isn't confirmed yet in `[square brackets]` so it shows as a placeholder.
6. Allergens: tag each ingredient (`tags: ['Milk', 'Soy?']`) and leave out `contains` and `check`; the book works them out from the tags.
7. If it is a dish on the guest menu, add its `id` to the right section in `menuOrder` at the top of `js/data.js`, in the spot where the menu prints it. A dish left out of `menuOrder` still shows, under "Other dishes". Batch recipes and components are not listed there.
8. Change `updated` at the top of the file to today's date.

## Testing

The private-ping handover check uses `L.waitFor()` so it runs under the live site's Content Security Policy.

The `tests/` folder holds click tests that open the book in Chrome and use it like the team does, on a phone, a tablet and a computer. Only the tests need installing (Node.js and Google Chrome on the computer):

```
cd tests
npm install
npm test
```

- **What it checks:** every page and admin tab at 390, 768 and 1280 px (no sideways scroll, no errors), and the click paths for the prep list (with double ups), orders, wastage, minutes, shift pings, guides (with a real PDF read by pdf.js), the admin panel and the recipe search. About 430 checks, a few minutes.
- **No real data.** The prep pages normally talk to Firebase. The tests answer those requests with a small stand-in (`tests/fake/`) that keeps made-up people (Sam, Ann and Bo), prep lines, orders, wastage and two guides in the browser. No kitchen PIN, staff names or real lines are needed, and nothing is written to the real book. pdf.js is installed with the tests (`pdfjs-dist`) and served from there, so the PDF test needs no internet.
- **Any time of day.** Each page opens at 14:00 today, kitchen time, and the clock runs from there, so the 10:30 shift pings popup and the rest behave the same whenever the tests run. The tests and the browser run in Queensland time whatever zone the computer is set to. The update check's answer carries no `Date` in the tests, so the book keeps that 14:00; `clock.test.js` sets a wrong phone clock and zone (`clock`, `tz` and `server` in `L.open`) to check the book still uses the server's time.
- `tests/wide-font.test.js` opens the top bar at 768 px with a wide fallback font forced (DejaVu Sans, what a Linux runner falls back to when the kitchen font is not loaded): the pills wrap inside the bar instead of pushing the Pings bell and the chef hat off the page.
- `tests/supplier-cutoff-readiness.test.js` holds the supplier tools request to check slow first-use completion: no order is marked before the catalogue lookup, linked suppliers open their sheet, a queued untick wins, and a failed read keeps the reminder. A denied lookup cannot complete an old chef's intent after another chef signs in, while the same chef keeps the fallback for unpublished supplier rules. The cut-off tests wait for the saved completion before checking the bar or reloading, including on the live site.
- `tests/ping-life.test.js` checks a ping's 30 minutes (24 Oct 2026): out of sight before its time, on at 10:30, gone at 11:00; a ping saved at 13:00 on until 13:30; Close does not bring it back, not even after a reload; removing a ping does not pop the rest up again; a repeat comes back the next day; a sheet left alone goes after 30 minutes; nothing over Who are you? or an Undo. The tests open at 14:00, so a ping seeded for them gets a time near then (`tm: 835`, 13:55).

## Good to know

- This repo is **public**, so anyone with the link can read the recipes. The page asks search engines not to list it.
- Allergens are worked out from the ingredients listed. Always check the supplier labels before answering a guest.
- Cook as written. Check with the head chef or sous chef before changing anything.
- **The book updates itself.** Every merge to `main` changes the site's `Last-Modified` and `ETag`. The book asks for just the headers of `index.html` (a tiny request, no Firestore) when it opens, when someone comes back to it and every 15 minutes while open. If they changed, it reloads: straight away when the app was brought back after 5 minutes or more away, otherwise once nobody has touched it for 2 minutes and nothing is being typed (an input in focus blocks it). Meanwhile a slim strip at the top of the screen says "New version available." with an Update now button; it sticks to the top while the page scrolls, and the bars that stick to the top of a page (the sync note, the recipe search, the admin nav) sit just under it (21 Oct 2026; it used to sit at the bottom with the toasts). Its words wrap inside it, and it grows when they need more room, as with Extra large text on a narrow phone in a font wider than the book's; the bars under it follow its real height (`--upd-h`, kept by `js/upkeep.js`, 28 Oct 2026). It reloads at most once every 10 minutes, so it cannot loop; the strip still offers Update now meanwhile, also when a newer version is found on coming back (28 Oct 2026; it used to show nothing then). The 04:00 daily refresh stays as a backstop. With no signal nothing reloads (27 Oct 2026): since 26 Oct 2026 (#387) the kitchen's data and any change not sent yet live only in the tab's memory, so a reload then would open an empty book and lose the waiting changes. Before any reload the book checks the browser's online flag and asks the server for the headers of `index.html` (`lkServerUp()` in `js/upkeep.js`, one tiny request, no Firestore, 8 seconds at most), so wifi with no internet counts as no signal too. The update check and this check share one request helper (`lkHead()`), and every answer also keeps the book's clock right. The 04:00 refresh and the catch-up after waking then try again 10 minutes later and 5 seconds after the signal is back. A refresh that had to wait has missed its night (28 Oct 2026), so from then on it reloads only by the automatic update's rule (`lkIdle()`: nothing in a box, half typed or waiting to send, and nobody has touched the book for 2 minutes): a chef typing in the recipe search mid-morning keeps the words and the keyboard (it used to reload under them once the signal came back). With no signal the automatic update waits for its next turn; after a tap Update now says "Checking the signal..." while it asks, then "Updating..." or "No signal. It updates later." The strip stays up while it waits, also when the book was just brought back and found no answer. Taps while it says "Checking the signal..." or "Updating..." are ignored. A tap while the automatic update runs, with the strip still saying "New version available.", takes that update over and says what it is doing, so the tap's own touch does not call it off (28 Oct 2026). The check and the files can take up to 8 seconds each, so the book looks again just before it reloads (28 Oct 2026): the 04:00 refresh waits 10 minutes more if something was typed or written meanwhile, the automatic update waits for its next turn if someone typed or wrote, and after 2 minutes untouched also if someone touched the book, with the strip still up, and Update now stops with "Not saved yet." and Tap again to update if something was typed after the tap. Coming back is a touch, so the update on coming back goes on over a swipe and still updates straight away (28 Oct 2026; a swipe used to call it off, and with a swipe every 45 seconds it never updated); a tick made meanwhile holds it back, and a touch in the last 600 ms makes the book look again 700 ms later, so a tap's tick holds it back too. The signal going while the files come in counts as no signal too: the service worker answers a failed file with the saved copy, so the browser's online flag is read again just before the reload. Each tries the reload once, so a reload the browser turned down is not tried again 8 seconds later, and only one 04:00 refresh runs at a time. While a change waits to send, Update now waits (head chef, 28 Oct 2026): it says "Not sent yet. It updates once sent.", starting with the words of the Not sent yet strip (`LK_PREP.unsent()`), a second tap does not get past it, and the book updates by itself once the change is sent: the tapped update runs at the next look (every 20 seconds) once nothing is in a box or half typed, even while the book is in use, within 10 minutes of the tap, and after that by the automatic update's rule (28 Oct 2026; it used to wait for 2 minutes untouched, so with a touch every 30 seconds it never updated). A tick made while Update now checks holds it back the same way. With something half typed it says "Not saved yet." and Tap again to update (4 seconds, like the other Tap again buttons). `tests/daily-refresh.test.js` checks the 04:00 reload, and that it waits 10 minutes while something is half written; `tests/offline-writes.test.js` the strip's fit in a wide font at 344, 360 and 390 px, the waits for the signal (wifi with no internet too), the look before the reload, a 04:00 refresh that missed its night, coming back over a swipe, Update now's wait and the second tap. A phone running a version from before this feature picks it up on its next reload (04:00 or reopening the app). No version number to bump and nothing to configure.
- **One clock.** Every time the book saves (written, ticked, ordered, removed, waste, sign-offs, guides) and every time it decides what day or hour it is (today's list, Waiting since, Closing soon, written after close, the 10:30 shift pings, 04:00) comes from the server's time in kitchen time (Queensland, UTC+10, no daylight saving). A phone's own clock can be minutes or hours out, or set to another zone. The answer to the update check above carries the server's time in its `Date` header, so the book keeps the gap on the phone (`lk.clock.gap`, kept for when there is no signal) and adds it. No extra requests and no Firestore reads. Before this, a phone 3 minutes slow could never undo its own mis-tap (the rules time the 2 minutes on the server). The rollovers wait until the clock has been checked on each visit (or 10 seconds), so a phone with the wrong date never rolls lines onto the wrong day. In the code: `LK_TIME` in `js/boot.js`; `now()`, `dayNow()` and `T.day`, `T.hour`, `T.mins`, `T.dow`, `T.clock`, `T.at` in `js/prep.js`. `Date.now()` is only for time passing on the phone (timers, the PIN lock, the idle sign-out). Times are still plain milliseconds, so old data and the cached past days need nothing.

- **Ticking on a busy day (22 Oct 2026).** A tick used to redraw the whole prep list 7 times inside the tap (the change, its toast and the tallies each drew the page); now it draws once at the end of the tap (`batch()` in `js/prep.js`), and when the server confirms a change only the "Not sent" marks are patched (`patchUnsent()`), not the whole page. Measured on an 80-line day on a phone 4 times slower than a laptop: 136 ms inside the tap down to 33 ms, and 178 ms until the screen is still down to 69 ms. `tests/prep-speed.test.js` checks one redraw per tick.
- **Firebase kept on the phone (22 Oct 2026).** The service worker keeps the Firebase files the prep pages load (from `www.gstatic.com/firebasejs/<version>/`) in the saved copy once they have loaded, and hands them out from there, like Google Fonts. Before, they were only in the browser's own cache, so a phone whose browser cache had been cleared could not open the prep list with no signal. Their address carries the version, so a kept file never goes out of date; a new Firebase version is a new address, and the old files go when `CACHE` in `sw.js` changes. In the click tests the worker's own requests go through the Firebase stand-in too (`PW_EXPERIMENTAL_SERVICE_WORKER_NETWORK_EVENTS`, set in `tests/lib.js`). `tests/saved-copy.test.js`.
- **Lighter on the phone (22 Oct 2026).** Opening the book no longer writes all its files to the phone's saved copy each time (about 2.4 MB per opening): only the files that changed are written, still all or nothing. The 04:00 refresh asks the server whether each file changed instead of downloading every file again (about 435 kB a phone each morning when nothing changed); an update found by the stamp check still downloads fresh. On a recipe, turning a tablet fits the drawing's pins once per frame, not on every resize event. `tests/saved-copy.test.js`.
- **Feels fast (21 Oct 2026).** Recipes opens about twice as fast on a phone: the shelves on The Pass and the groups on Tiles that are off screen are not drawn until they scroll near (`content-visibility` in `css/app.css`). Coming back from a recipe puts the plate you opened back at the same height on the screen (`backHome()` in `js/app.js`), because the old scroll position would land on the wrong shelf. Buttons, links and folds give way a little (scale 0.97) the moment a finger is on them, so a tap feels answered before the next page is drawn; pins on a plate keep their place, and with reduced motion a press fades a little instead. `tests/feels-fast.test.js`. Measured on a phone four times slower than a laptop: Recipes from 0.45 to 0.79 s down to about 0.33 s; the landing page (0.18 s) and a recipe card (0.24 s) were already quick from the saved copy.

### Recipes changed in the book (21 Oct 2026)

The head chef can change and add recipes in the book itself, not only in `js/data.js` (Manage Recipes & Drawings, below). The changes are kept in Firestore and every phone with the kitchen PIN lays them over `js/data.js`.

- **Who:** only the head chef writes them, with their Google account (while nobody else is signed in with a PIN on that device) or with their own PIN as the person set as Head chef (`config/main.headId`). Sous chefs, leads and staff only read. The rules check it on the server (`bookBy()` in `firestore.rules`), so a straight write from a staff phone is refused too.
- **Data:** `book/index` lists each changed recipe and its version (`r: { id: v }`), each drawing made in the book and its version (`d`), and, once the head chef changes it, the guest menu order (`menu`). `recipes/<id>` holds the recipe in the same shape as `js/data.js` (`r`), its version `v`, `upd`, `by`, `src` (`book` for a recipe from `js/data.js`, `new` for one made in the book), `base` (a fingerprint of the `js/data.js` copy it was made from, so a later change to the file can be spotted) `removed` (hides it; recipes are never deleted) and `off` (the head chef went back to the `js/data.js` copy; the record stays as an earlier version). Each save keeps the version before it in `recipes/<id>/hist/<v>` (`was`, `at`), which nobody can change. The version goes up by one with every save and the index must say the same version in the same batch, so a save made from an old copy is refused.
- **Reads kept small:** a phone reads `book/index` (one read) when the book connects, when someone comes back after 30 minutes away and when the recipes are opened, each time only when the last check is 30 minutes old, then reads only the recipes whose version changed. No listener, no polling. `rxSync()` in `js/prep.js`.
- **On the phone:** the changes are kept in `lk.rx` (tied to the kitchen PIN; a new or wrong PIN drops them, with the guides), so the next start draws them straight away, before Firebase connects. `js/overlay.js` runs after `js/data.js` and before `js/prep.js` and `js/app.js`: it keeps a clean copy of `js/data.js` (`LK_RX.pristine(id)`) and builds fresh recipe objects from it each time, because `js/app.js` adds worked-out fields (allergens from the tags, the menu section) to the recipe objects. `js/app.js` works its index out again (`reindex()`) when new changes come in (`lk-rx`), and on the recipe list only the results are redrawn.
- **The book's copy wins:** once a recipe is changed in the book, phones with the kitchen PIN show the book's copy, whatever `js/data.js` says later. Phones without the kitchen PIN (someone who scanned the QR sign) show `js/data.js`.
- **Safety:** a change that does not look like a recipe (no name, a kind the book does not know) is left out and the book's own copy stays. If the book fails to draw with the changes, the next start shows `js/data.js` alone (`lk.rx.bad`) until new changes come in.
- **Tests:** `tests/overlay.test.js` (changed, new, hidden and broken recipes, the next start, reads, another phone's change, a failed start, no kitchen PIN, a staff write refused); the rules checks in `tests/rules/rules.test.mjs` (the head chef by PIN and by Google allowed; a sous chef, staff, a spoofed writer, a wrong PIN, an old copy, an index that disagrees, unknown fields, deletes and changed earlier versions refused); the lock probe also checks that a recipe and the index cannot be written with only the kitchen PIN.

### Staff lock
- **PIN sessions (22 Oct 2026, review S8).** Every phone signs in to Firebase anonymously, so it has its own id (uid), kept by Firebase on the phone. Typing a personal PIN first writes a try (`tries/<person>`: the PIN's fingerprint, the time and the phone's uid). The rules allow one try per person every 20 seconds, and a wrong try still counts, so a PIN cannot be guessed by script: 10,000 PINs take over two days per person, and the page's own wait after 3 wrong tries still applies.
