# Leichhardt Kitchen Book

The Leichhardt Hotel kitchen's app: the recipe book, the prep list, the order list, the wastage log and the team's how-to guides, plus the head chef's admin panel. It works on any phone, tablet or computer with a web browser and can be installed as an app. Working rules for changing it are in `CLAUDE.md`.

## What it does

- **Two ways to look at the recipes.** The Pass opens first. The Pass / Tiles switch next to the search box changes it any time (and **Settings** sets which one opens):
  - **Tiles:** every recipe as a card with its small drawn dish, with search, recipe type and "Free from" filters on top.
  - **The Pass:** dishes drawn as plates and batch recipes as GN pans. Tap a plate to zoom in, then tap the laksa on the barramundi to open the laksa. The allergen lens shows where an allergen is at every level.
- **Linked batch recipes.** Any ingredient that has its own recipe is underlined orange with a "Recipe 06" tag, so tapping it opens that recipe. Batch recipes show which dishes they go on.
- **Allergens roll up.** A dish picks up the allergens of the batch recipes it uses, and names where each one comes from. A "?" means "may be in it, check the supplier label".
- **Cards to come.** A batch recipe that isn't in the book yet shows as "Card to come". Every dish that uses one is marked "Allergens incomplete", is left out of the Free from filter, and counts as "may have" under the allergen lens, until that card is added.
- **Set out like the guest menu.** Recipes are grouped in the order the menu prints them: Snackin' Plates, Salads, Big Country Feeds, Off the Grill, Toppers, Schnittys & Parmys, Hand Held, Sides, Dessert, Seniors and Kids, then the Functions platters. Tiles and The Pass both follow it, and each section is also a filter chip in Tiles. The Functions chip is a rounded rectangle and always sits first after All. Batch recipes and components come after the menu sections ("Bains and prep" on The Pass).
- **From the side.** Plated dishes also show a labelled side view, so the team can see what sits on top. Burgers show their build, top to bottom, instead.
- **Pin labels.** On the plate drawing the page places each label where it does not cover the food, other labels or other pins, with a thin line back to its pin. It reads the drawing to find the food, so a new dish needs no label tuning. Boards and platters (more than 8 pins) use smaller labels. The ring is see-through so the food shows through it.
- **Manage Recipes & Drawings (head chef only, 21 Oct 2026).** The head chef changes any recipe or adds a new one in the book (chef hat menu, or Change this recipe on a card). Staff phones show the change within half an hour, or straight away when the book opens. See Manage Recipes & Drawings below.
- **Functions (21 Oct 2026).** A function manager books a function once (chef hat, Functions): the date, event start, guests, platters from the book's Functions section, and when they go out. The kitchen sees it in a band under the top bar on every page on the day, with the next round, and taps it for the full timetable and the kitchen comment. See Functions below.
- **Tick off steps** while cooking. The ticks clear when the page is closed.
- **Search (October 2026):**
  - Every word counts, in any order: "parmy chicken" finds Chicken Parmy.
  - "Gluten free", "gf", "no dairy", "df", "nut free", "egg free", "shellfish free" and so on show the recipes free from it, by the same rule as the Free from chips (no "may have", and no recipe with a card still to come). A note above the results says so. It also works with a word: "gf burger". So the Pass, which has the allergen lens instead of Free from chips, can do free-from too.
  - A word that is one slip away from a word in the book is put right when nothing else matches ("chiken" shows chicken, with a note saying so). Searches that still find nothing are queued for the admin panel as before.
  - The search bar stays at the top of the screen while the list scrolls. On phones the "16 of 114 match" count shows while a search or filter is on. Switching between the Pass and Tiles starts at the top of the list.
- **Recipe cards on phones and tablets:** the drawings come first, so a row under the name trail jumps straight to Allergens, Ingredients or Method. It is hidden on wide screens, where the card is in two columns.
- **Settings** (the chef hat at the top right, then Settings):
  - Which view it opens with: The Pass and Tiles each show a small drawing of the recipes page on a computer and on a phone (`miniShot()` in `js/app.js`), so staff see the layout before they pick. The one in use says "Opens with this".
  - Text size (Normal, Large, Extra large)
  - Head chef only (shows only on a device where the head chef is signed in, see Admin has one home): Clear ticked steps, and Shared kitchen device (Personal phone or Shared device): on a shared device the prep pages ask who you are again after 10 minutes idle and each new day; see Shared kitchen device under Staff lock. The choice stays on the device after the head chef signs out.
  - Open on a phone (computers and wide tablets only, 900 px and wider): the QR code (`qr.svg`) that opens the book, with Print the A4 sign (`qr-sign.pdf`) and Download the QR code (`qr.png`)

  Settings are saved on each phone or tablet.

## Adding or changing a recipe

All recipes are in **`js/data.js`**. The top of that file explains every field. Recipes the head chef changes or adds in the book itself are kept in Firestore and laid over this file, and the book's copy wins (see Recipes changed in the book below). To add one here:

1. Copy an existing recipe block, from `{` to `},`.
2. Give it a new `id` (short, no spaces, for example `gravy`) and the next card number.
3. Fill in the name, yield, ingredients, steps and allergens.
4. To make an ingredient tappable, add `link: 'gravy'` (the other recipe's `id`).
5. Put anything that isn't confirmed yet in `[square brackets]` so it shows as a placeholder.
6. Allergens: tag each ingredient (`tags: ['Milk', 'Soy?']`) and leave out `contains` and `check`; the book works them out from the tags.
7. If it is a dish on the guest menu, add its `id` to the right section in `menuOrder` at the top of `js/data.js`, in the spot where the menu prints it. A dish left out of `menuOrder` still shows, under "Other dishes". Batch recipes and components are not listed there.
8. Change `updated` at the top of the file to today's date.

## Testing

The `tests/` folder holds click tests that open the book in Chrome and use it like the team does, on a phone, a tablet and a computer. Only the tests need installing (Node.js and Google Chrome on the computer):

```
cd tests
npm install
npm test
```

- **What it checks:** every page and admin tab at 390, 768 and 1280 px (no sideways scroll, no errors), and the click paths for the prep list (with double ups), orders, wastage, minutes, shift pings, guides (with a real PDF read by pdf.js), the admin panel and the recipe search. About 430 checks, a few minutes.
- **No real data.** The prep pages normally talk to Firebase. The tests answer those requests with a small stand-in (`tests/fake/`) that keeps made-up people (Sam, Ann and Bo), prep lines, orders, wastage and two guides in the browser. No kitchen PIN, staff names or real lines are needed, and nothing is written to the real book. pdf.js is installed with the tests (`pdfjs-dist`) and served from there, so the PDF test needs no internet.
- **Any time of day.** Each page opens at 14:00 today, kitchen time, and the clock runs from there, so the 10:30 shift pings popup and the rest behave the same whenever the tests run. The tests and the browser run in Queensland time whatever zone the computer is set to. The update check's answer carries no `Date` in the tests, so the book keeps that 14:00; `clock.test.js` sets a wrong phone clock and zone (`clock`, `tz` and `server` in `L.open`) to check the book still uses the server's time.
- `tests/wide-font.test.js` opens the top bar at 768 px with a wide fallback font forced (DejaVu Sans, what a Linux runner falls back to when the kitchen font is not loaded): the pills wrap inside the bar instead of pushing the Pings bell and the chef hat off the page.
- `tests/keep.test.js` holds what already works well and must not break while pages change around it (the usability work from 20 Oct 2026): "Logged often" fills item, unit, reason and section; the reason sheet closes with Escape; Back walks out of a sub-recipe and the trail and Zoom out link back; a long admin list shows 15, then more, then fewer, and its search keeps the cursor; a wastage day that cannot be read does not break the page. `failReads` in `L.open` (a regex) makes the stand-in's `getDoc` and `getDocs` fail for the paths that match, like a read lost to a bad signal.
- `tests/minutes-version.test.js` publishes minutes as the head chef, changes them as a new version and checks that version 2 is published, version 1 is kept as it was under `hist`, and the second tap is asked for.
- `npm test -- recipes` runs only the files with "recipes" in the name. `SHOTS=1 npm test -- pages` also saves a screenshot of every page to `tests/shots/`. `BASE=https://kuschikuschbert.github.io/leichhardt-kitchen/ npm test` runs the tests on the live site (Firebase is still the stand-in). `CHROME=/path/to/chromium` uses another browser. Up to six files run at once (two more than the computer has processors), all in one Chrome that `run.js` starts, each page in its own fresh profile with its own stand-in; `JOBS=1 npm test` runs one at a time. The tests do not sit out fixed pauses (21 Oct 2026): `L.settle(p, ms)` waits at most `ms` but stops as soon as the page has gone still, nothing is loading and no timer is due inside those `ms`, and `L.open` also waits for the kitchen's settings to be read. A click or wait that has not worked in 10 s fails (Playwright's own limit is 30 s). The whole run went from about 3.6 minutes to 1.4 on a four-processor computer. For something that comes in later than the page goes still, wait for the thing itself with `L.waitFor`. Each file's output prints in one piece when it ends, with its time, and the five slowest files are listed at the end.
- **Which pictures changed.** Before a change to the look, keep a copy of `tests/shots/` (for example as `tests/shots-baseline/` or `tests/shots-baseline-u/`, which git ignores). After the change, `SHOTS=1 npm test -- pages` again, then `node tests/shots-diff.js tests/shots-baseline` lists every page whose picture is not the same as before, so only those need a look.
- `node tests/serve.js` serves the book at http://127.0.0.1:8777/ for trying it by hand.
- **On GitHub.** The same tests run on every pull request to `main` as the **Click tests** check (`.github/workflows/pr-tests.yml`, Chrome on the runner, screenshots kept as an artifact when a run fails), so a pull request cannot merge until they pass. Since 22 Oct 2026 the same check also runs `node --check` on `js/lib/*.js`, the rules checks on the Firestore emulator (`tests/rules`) and the nightly learning check (`scripts/emulator-check.mjs`), so a rules change cannot merge without them. After each Pages deploy the **Live click tests** workflow runs them again against the live site.

## Good to know

- This repo is **public**, so anyone with the link can read the recipes. The page asks search engines not to list it.
- Allergens are worked out from the ingredients listed. Always check the supplier labels before answering a guest.
- Cook as written. Check with the head chef or sous chef before changing anything.
- **The book updates itself.** Every merge to `main` changes the site's `Last-Modified` and `ETag`. The book asks for just the headers of `index.html` (a tiny request, no Firestore) when it opens, when someone comes back to it and every 15 minutes while open. If they changed, it reloads: straight away when the app was brought back after 5 minutes or more away, otherwise once nobody has touched it for 2 minutes and nothing is being typed (an input in focus blocks it). Meanwhile a slim strip at the top of the screen says "New version available." with an Update now button; it sticks to the top while the page scrolls, and the bars that stick to the top of a page (the sync note, the recipe search, the admin nav) sit just under it (21 Oct 2026; it used to sit at the bottom with the toasts). It reloads at most once every 10 minutes, so it cannot loop. The 04:00 daily refresh stays as a backstop. A phone running a version from before this feature picks it up on its next reload (04:00 or reopening the app). No version number to bump and nothing to configure.
- **One clock.** Every time the book saves (written, ticked, ordered, removed, waste, sign-offs, guides) and every time it decides what day or hour it is (today's list, Waiting since, Closing soon, written after close, the 10:30 shift pings, 04:00) comes from the server's time in kitchen time (Queensland, UTC+10, no daylight saving). A phone's own clock can be minutes or hours out, or set to another zone. The answer to the update check above carries the server's time in its `Date` header, so the book keeps the gap on the phone (`lk.clock.gap`, kept for when there is no signal) and adds it. No extra requests and no Firestore reads. Before this, a phone 3 minutes slow could never undo its own mis-tap (the rules time the 2 minutes on the server). The rollovers wait until the clock has been checked on each visit (or 10 seconds), so a phone with the wrong date never rolls lines onto the wrong day. In the code: `LK_TIME` in `index.html`; `now()`, `dayNow()` and `T.day`, `T.hour`, `T.mins`, `T.dow`, `T.clock`, `T.at` in `js/prep.js`. `Date.now()` is only for time passing on the phone (timers, the PIN lock, the idle sign-out). Times are still plain milliseconds, so old data and the cached past days need nothing.

- **Lighter on the phone (22 Oct 2026).** Opening the book no longer writes all its files to the phone's saved copy each time (about 2.4 MB per opening): only the files that changed are written, still all or nothing. The 04:00 refresh asks the server whether each file changed instead of downloading every file again (about 435 kB a phone each morning when nothing changed); an update found by the stamp check still downloads fresh. On a recipe, turning a tablet fits the drawing's pins once per frame, not on every resize event. `tests/saved-copy.test.js`.
- **Feels fast (21 Oct 2026).** Recipes opens about twice as fast on a phone: the shelves on The Pass and the groups on Tiles that are off screen are not drawn until they scroll near (`content-visibility` in `css/app.css`). Coming back from a recipe puts the plate you opened back at the same height on the screen (`backHome()` in `js/app.js`), because the old scroll position would land on the wrong shelf. Buttons, links and folds give way a little (scale 0.97) the moment a finger is on them, so a tap feels answered before the next page is drawn; pins on a plate keep their place, and with reduced motion a press fades a little instead. `tests/feels-fast.test.js`. Measured on a phone four times slower than a laptop: Recipes from 0.45 to 0.79 s down to about 0.33 s; the landing page (0.18 s) and a recipe card (0.24 s) were already quick from the saved copy.

## Installing it as an app

- The logo is a chalk "KB": off-white handwriting (Caveat) with an orange underline on a dark specials board, chosen by the head chef from five options (18 Oct 2026). The letters are drawn as paths, so no font is needed. `icons/` holds the app icons (512 with "kitchen book" and a flame; 192, 180 and 96 with just KB; the maskable one drawn smaller for Android's circle crop), `icon.svg` is the browser tab version, and the same mark sits in the top bar (`LOGO_MARK` in `js/app.js`) and on the QR sign. Phones that installed the app earlier may need it removed and added again to show the new icon (iPhone always does).
- The book is an installable web app: `manifest.webmanifest` (name, colours, icons in `icons/`, shortcuts to Prep list, Orders, Wastage and Recipes on a long press of the icon) and `sw.js` (service worker).
- Phones, tablets and computers that have not installed the book yet see an **Install the app** card at the top of the landing page (18 Oct 2026; computers from 20 Oct 2026). In Chrome, Edge and Samsung Internet it has an Install button; on iPhone and iPad it explains Share, then Add to Home Screen; on a Windows or Mac computer without the button it points to the install icon at the right of the address bar (in Edge, Apps, Install this site as an app), in Safari on a Mac it says File, Add to Dock, and in Firefox on a computer it says to use Edge or Chrome, because Firefox cannot install apps there; elsewhere it points to the browser menu. "Not now" hides it on that device only (`installNo` in the saved settings). It never shows in the installed app.
- Settings has the same **Install the app** section with the same wording for each device (`computerSteps()` in `js/app.js` is the computer text for both). Click tests: `tests/install-card.test.js` (Windows Chrome, Edge and Firefox, Mac Safari and Chrome, iPhone).
- The browser's install offer (`beforeinstallprompt`) is caught by a small script in `index.html` before the app scripts load, and `preventDefault()` is called on it, because Chrome only opens its install box from our button once that has been done. If the box fails to open, the card and Settings fall back to the manual steps. The manifest `id` is `/leichhardt-kitchen/`, so the app has its own identity on `kuschikuschbert.github.io` and cannot clash with another app on the same address.
- The service worker opens the book from the copy saved on the phone (21 Oct 2026; it was network first before), so the book opens at once even on weak kitchen wifi, and with no signal at all. Each time the book opens, the worker asks the server in the background whether any of the book's files changed (small "has it changed?" requests). Only when every file came in is the saved copy replaced, so a phone never mixes old and new files. If something changed it tells the open book: in the first 8 seconds after opening, before anyone has touched the screen, the book reloads straight into the new version; later it shows the "New version available" bar and updates the same way as before (straight away when brought back, otherwise after 2 minutes untouched, or with Update now). The update and the 04:00 refresh fetch their files with the `X-LK-Fresh` header, which the worker sends to the network first. Google Fonts are kept once loaded. Firebase (prep list, orders, wastage) is not touched; it keeps its own offline copy. Other files (icons, the QR code) are kept once loaded: change `CACHE` in `sw.js` when they change, or to force everyone onto a fresh copy. `tests/saved-copy.test.js` (its own copy of the test server, with every file held back 4 s, then a changed `js/data.js`).

- **Who:** only the head chef writes them, with their Google account (while nobody else is signed in with a PIN on that device) or with their own PIN as the person set as Head chef (`config/main.headId`). - **Data:** `book/index` lists each changed recipe and its version (`r: { id: v }`), each drawing made in the book and its version (`d`), and, once the head chef changes it, the guest menu order (`menu`). `recipes/<id>` holds the recipe in the same shape as `js/data.js` (`r`), its version `v`, `upd`, `by`, `src` (`book` for a recipe from `js/data.js`, `new` for one made in the book), `base` (a fingerprint of the `js/data.js` copy it was made from, so a later change to the file can be spotted) `removed` (hides it; recipes are never deleted) and `off` (the head chef went back to the `js/data.js` copy; the record stays as an earlier version). Each save keeps the version before it in `recipes/<id>/hist/<v>` (`was`, `at`), which nobody can change. The version goes up by one with every save and the index must say the same version in the same batch, so a save made from an old copy is refused.
- **Reads kept small:** a phone reads `book/index` (one read) when the book connects, when someone comes back after 30 minutes away and when the recipes are opened, each time only when the last check is 30 minutes old, then reads only the recipes whose version changed. No listener, no polling. `rxSync()` in `js/prep.js`.
- **On the phone:** the changes are kept in `lk.rx` (tied to the kitchen PIN; a new or wrong PIN drops them, with the guides), so the next start draws them straight away, before Firebase connects. `js/overlay.js` runs after `js/data.js` and before `js/prep.js` and `js/app.js`: it keeps a clean copy of `js/data.js` (`LK_RX.pristine(id)`) and builds fresh recipe objects from it each time, because `js/app.js` adds worked-out fields (allergens from the tags, the menu section) to the recipe objects. `js/app.js` works its index out again (`reindex()`) when new changes come in (`lk-rx`), and on the recipe list only the results are redrawn.
- **The book's copy wins:** once a recipe is changed in the book, phones with the kitchen PIN show the book's copy, whatever `js/data.js` says later. Phones without the kitchen PIN (someone who scanned the QR sign) show `js/data.js`.
- **Safety:** a change that does not look like a recipe (no name, a kind the book does not know) is left out and the book's own copy stays. If the book fails to draw with the changes, the next start shows `js/data.js` alone (`lk.rx.bad`) until new changes come in.
- **Tests:** `tests/overlay.test.js` (changed, new, hidden and broken recipes, the next start, reads, another phone's change, a failed start, no kitchen PIN, a staff write refused); the rules checks in `tests/rules/rules.test.mjs` (the head chef by PIN and by Google allowed; a sous chef, staff, a spoofed writer, a wrong PIN, an old copy, an index that disagrees, unknown fields, deletes and changed earlier versions refused); the lock probe also checks that a recipe and the index cannot be written with only the kitchen PIN.

### Proofreader

- Every new written line is tidied (spacing, punctuation, capital first letter) and spell-checked before it is saved, in the browser, no service needed.

### Nightly backup (on the server)

- Every night at 03:30 kitchen time a GitHub Action (`.github/workflows/nightly-backup.yml`) runs `scripts/nightly-backup.mjs` (19 Oct 2026). It reads the book the way the admin panel's Backup button does and writes one JSON file in the same shape: people, prep lines, order lines, wastage and end-of-day reviews from the last 45 days (older days are in earlier backups and never change), every shift ping with who has seen it, every meeting minutes with earlier versions and sign-offs, who worked which kitchen section, what the prep and order lists have learned, the learning log, tallies, sections, empty searches, card opens, the guides' words and small pictures, the head chef's recipe changes with their earlier versions, and who has set a PIN. PIN fingerprints and the guides' page pictures are left out, like the button. It reads only (about 45 days of lists plus one read per record elsewhere) and writes nothing.
- The file is encrypted on the runner with the `BACKUP_KEY` secret (openssl, AES-256, PBKDF2) and kept as the run's artifact for 90 days. The repository is public and anyone signed in to GitHub can download an artifact, so the clear file never leaves the runner. It needs three repository secrets: `FIREBASE_SA` and `KITCHEN_PIN` (the nightly learning has them) and `BACKUP_KEY`, a long random passphrase (16 characters or more; keep a copy in a password manager, because a lost key means unreadable backups). Without the key the run stops before reading anything.
- To read a backup: Actions tab, Nightly backup, open a run, download the artifact, unzip it, then on a computer with openssl (a Mac has it): `openssl enc -d -aes-256-cbc -pbkdf2 -iter 200000 -in kitchen-book-<date>.json.enc -out kitchen-book-<date>.json` and type the passphrase. The result is the same file the Backup button saves. There is no restore button: putting something back is done by hand from the file, or with a script written for the case when it comes.
- Test it on the Firestore emulator: `cd scripts && npm install`, then from `tests/rules`: `./node_modules/.bin/firebase emulators:exec --only firestore --project demo-test "node rules.test.mjs && KITCHEN_PIN=000000 BACKUP_DAYS=5 node ../../scripts/nightly-backup.mjs /tmp/backup.json"` (the rules tests seed the emulator first).

### Security setup

- `firestore.rules` is the rules template. The live rules are pasted into the Firebase console with the real kitchen PIN and owner email filled in. Never commit those values.
- To publish: the **Publish rules** workflow in the Actions tab (Run workflow). Or paste a filled-in copy into the Firebase console, Firestore Database, Rules, Publish. The workflow is what a cloud session uses: the real values live in GitHub's secrets, the Firebase console and on the head chef's computer, never in the repo.
- **The lock is proved after every publish (19 Oct 2026).** The Publish rules workflow ends by running `tests/rules/probe.mjs` against the live book with the public web settings and the `KITCHEN_PIN` secret: a read of `config/main` with the kitchen PIN must work (so a dead connection, or a secret that does not match the published rules, cannot pass as "all refused"), and a wrong kitchen PIN, a read of a PIN hash or a proof, a prep line carrying a writer id with no PIN proof, a change to the config and a planted PIN hash must all come back permission denied. The **Lock probe** workflow runs the same probe every Monday at 04:00 kitchen time and on demand, so a rule changed by hand in the console is caught within the week. Nothing is written (every write it tries is refused) and the PIN is never printed. `tests/rules/probe-checks.mjs` holds the checks; `probe.test.mjs` runs them on the emulator as part of `npm test` there, against the right PIN and a wrong one, and `probe.mjs` itself runs against the emulator with `FIRESTORE_EMULATOR_HOST` set. The probe talks to Firestore over plain HTTPS (the REST API, no SDK), the rules apply the same way, and when the `APPCHECK_DEBUG_TOKEN` secret is set it swaps it for an App Check token first and sends it with every request, so the probe keeps working once App Check is enforced (19 Oct 2026). The debug token is made in the Firebase console (App Check, Apps, the web app's menu, Manage debug tokens). Since the Browser key only works from the book's own address (22 Oct 2026), the probe sends that address (`Referer`) on every call; without it the token exchange is refused with "Requests from referer <empty> are blocked", and the probe's message now shows Google's reason.
- The Firebase web key in `js/firebase-config.js` is public by design. Restrict it in Google Cloud (Credentials, the Browser key) to the site address, and use App Check so only the real site can talk to the database. The web app is registered for App Check with Fraud Defense (reCAPTCHA Enterprise) since 19 Oct 2026, and `appCheckEnterpriseKey` in `js/firebase-config.js` is its site key (public too), so every phone sends a token with each request; `appCheckKey` still works for an older reCAPTCHA v3 key. Enforce is on for Cloud Firestore since 22 Oct 2026 (App Check, APIs, Cloud Firestore): nothing but the real site gets through. ### Staff lock
- **PIN entry.** The kitchen PIN and a person's own PIN go in by themselves as soon as the fourth digit is typed. When choosing a new PIN, the cursor moves to "Type it again" after the first four digits and sends after the second four. Wrong tries still count toward the lock. The PIN box takes the keyboard by itself (19 Oct 2026): it has focus as soon as the screen shows, with the number pad on a phone, a tablet or a Windows touch screen and straight typing on a computer, and the page heading does not take the focus back. An iPhone opens the keyboard only inside a tap, so the own PIN box is already on the screen while the book checks whether the person has a PIN; a PIN typed in that moment goes in once the book knows (`pin-auto`, `focusPin()` in `js/prep.js`; `tests/pin-keyboard.test.js`).
- **Proofreader.** It runs on prep lines, order lines and wastage items. It never changes a word it already knows, the singular or plural of a known word, a past tense of one, or a word with an accent (jalapeño). Orders and wastage now tell the writer what was corrected (prep still has the Undo button). Add everyday words to `KITCHEN` in `js/prep.js` when it wrongly changes one.
- **Desktop idle lock.** On a computer (mouse and keyboard), 7.5 minutes with no mouse, key or touch sends the prep pages back to the person's own PIN screen. The kitchen PIN is not asked again. Phones and tablets are not timed. A page that was left closed for longer than that also asks for the own PIN when it opens. It uses a timer and a timestamp on the device (`lk.prep.seen`), no reads or writes. Change `IDLE_MS` in `js/prep.js`.
- **Shared kitchen device (19 Oct 2026).** Settings, "Shared kitchen device", shown only on the head chef's signed-in device (20 Oct 2026, with Clear all ticks; `tests/admin-hidden.test.js`), kept on that device only (`shared` in `lk.settings.v1`). For a tablet or phone everyone picks up: after 10 minutes with no touch (any device, not only computers), and when the book opens on a new kitchen day, the prep pages go back to "Who are you?" rather than the last person's PIN, so nobody writes under the last name or sees that person's pings and minutes. The kitchen PIN stays, so the recipes, guides and lists are never locked, and the own PIN screen says "This device asks who you are again after 10 minutes" instead of "This phone stays signed in". A personal phone (the default) keeps the person signed in as before. `SHARED_MS`, `shared()`, `dayExpired()` in `js/prep.js`; the sign-in day is `lk.prep.day`. Tests in `tests/shared-device.test.js`.
- **Sign out on a shared kitchen device (22 Oct 2026)** takes away what that person left on it: function drafts (they hold a customer's phone and email), recipe and drawing drafts, and the mail they sent from it (`forgetMine()` in `js/prep.js`). Sign out still asks first while something is unsaved. The idle lock keeps them, so nothing typed is lost to a pause; a personal phone keeps them too. `tests/page-roles.test.js`.
- **Nobody signed in on a shared device (20 Oct 2026).** After the 10 minutes, or on a new day, the prep list, orders and wastage still show, with a "Nobody is signed in on this device" bar and a Sign in button. A tap that writes (tick, add, urgent, remove, Undo, order or waste lines) or Add asks "Who are you?" first ("Then it is done for you"), and once the PIN is right the same tap is done under that person, so nobody taps twice. What was typed in the add boxes stays. "Who are you?" lists the people who signed in on that device today first, under "Here today" (`lk.prep.here`), and has Back to the list. Reading still needs only the kitchen PIN and every write still needs a person's PIN, as before. A personal phone is unchanged. `guest()`, `guestAsk()`, `guestReplay()`, `GUEST_WRITES` in `js/prep.js`; `tests/shared-tablet.test.js`.
- **Wrong PINs count per person (20 Oct 2026).** The wait after 3 wrong PINs is kept per name (`lk.prep.lock.<id>`), so one cook's typos never lock the rest of the team out of a shared tablet. The old shared count moves to the first person who types a PIN after the update, once.
- **Resume runs once per page load** (20 Oct 2026), also when nobody was signed in. Before, a device opened with nobody signed in could sign a person out halfway through typing their PIN when the config changed.
- Each write sends the writer's id (`by`) and, in the same batch, `proofs/<id>` with `tok` (a hash of the person's own PIN) and `at` (a server timestamp). The rules compare `tok` with `staffpins/<id>.hash`, which nobody can read, using `getAfter`, and require `at` to equal the request time, so a proof left over from an earlier write cannot be reused. A wrong or missing PIN is refused by the server.
- The first person to set a PIN for a name claims it. The owner can reset it (Reset PIN) and it is chosen again.
- Limits: someone with the kitchen PIN could still guess a 4-digit PIN online by script (the page makes people wait after 3 wrong tries, 1 to 30 minutes and kept on the phone, but a script skips the page; App Check, enforced since 22 Oct 2026, stops plain scripts but not a real browser; sign-in sessions with a try limit are planned), and add, tick and remove rights per person are checked in the page only (who wrote a line, who ticked it and which days staff write are checked by the rules).
