## Adding or changing a recipe

All recipes are in **`js/data.js`**. The top of that file explains every field. Recipes the head chef changes or adds in the book itself are kept in Firestore and laid over this file, and the book's copy wins (see Recipes changed in the book below). To add one here:

1. Copy an existing recipe block, from `{` to `},`.
2. Give it a new `id` (short, no spaces, for example `gravy`) and the next card number.
3. Fill in the name, yield, ingredients, steps and allergens.
4. To make an ingredient tappable, add `link: 'gravy'` (the other recipe's `id`).
5. Put anything that isn't confirmed yet in `[square brackets]` so it shows as a placeholder.
6. Allergens: tag each ingredient (`tags: ['Milk', 'Soy?']`) and leave out `contains` and `check`; the book works them out from the tags.
7. If it is a dish on the guest menu, add its `id` to the right section in `menuOrder` at the top of `js/data.js`, in the spot where the menu prints it. A dish left out of `menuOrder` still shows, under "Other dishes". Batch recipes and components are not listed there.
8. Change `updated` at the top of the file to today's date.

In the code: `LK_TIME` in `js/boot.js`; `now()`, `dayNow()` and `T.day`, `T.hour`, `T.mins`, `T.dow`, `T.clock`, `T.at` in `js/prep.js`. `Date.now()` is only for time passing on the phone (timers, the PIN lock, the idle sign-out). Times are still plain milliseconds, so old data and the cached past days need nothing.

- **Ticking on a busy day (22 Oct 2026).** A tick used to redraw the whole prep list 7 times inside the tap (the change, its toast and the tallies each drew the page); now it draws once at the end of the tap (`batch()` in `js/prep.js`), and when the server confirms a change only the "Not sent" marks are patched (`patchUnsent()`), not the whole page. Measured on an 80-line day on a phone 4 times slower than a laptop: 136 ms inside the tap down to 33 ms, and 178 ms until the screen is still down to 69 ms. `tests/prep-speed.test.js` checks one redraw per tick.
- **Firebase kept on the phone (22 Oct 2026).** The service worker keeps the Firebase files the prep pages load (from `www.gstatic.com/firebasejs/<version>/`) in the saved copy once they have loaded, and hands them out from there, like Google Fonts. Before, they were only in the browser's own cache, so a phone whose browser cache had been cleared could not open the prep list with no signal. Their address carries the version, so a kept file never goes out of date; a new Firebase version is a new address, and the current generation cleanup retains the `lk-book-v8` Firebase/font/lazy-module bag. Changing that bag needs an explicit compatible migration. In the click tests the worker's own requests go through the Firebase stand-in too (`PW_EXPERIMENTAL_SERVICE_WORKER_NETWORK_EVENTS`, set in `tests/lib.js`). `tests/saved-copy.test.js`.
- **Lighter on the phone (22 Oct 2026).** The October implementation reduced saved-copy writes (about 2.4 MB per opening before that change). Since 2 November 2026 an unchanged shell round skips file writes; a changed round prepares a complete immutable generation, including unchanged members. The earlier byte figures below describe the former implementation. The 04:00 refresh asks the server whether each file changed instead of downloading every file again (about 435 kB a phone each morning when nothing changed); an update found by the stamp check asks the same way since 28 Oct 2026, through the worker's round (with no worker in charge it still downloads every file fresh). On a recipe, turning a tablet fits the drawing's pins once per frame, not on every resize event. `tests/saved-copy.test.js`.
- **Feels fast (21 Oct 2026).** Recipes opens about twice as fast on a phone: the shelves on The Pass and the groups on Tiles that are off screen are not drawn until they scroll near (`content-visibility` in `css/app.css`). Coming back from a recipe puts the plate you opened back at the same height on the screen (`backHome()` in `js/app.js`), because the old scroll position would land on the wrong shelf. Buttons, links and folds give way a little (scale 0.97) the moment a finger is on them, so a tap feels answered before the next page is drawn; pins on a plate keep their place, and with reduced motion a press fades a little instead. `tests/feels-fast.test.js`. Measured on a phone four times slower than a laptop: Recipes from 0.45 to 0.79 s down to about 0.33 s; the landing page (0.18 s) and a recipe card (0.24 s) were already quick from the saved copy. Answer the tap first (4 Nov 2026, `restDraw()` in `js/app.js`, `tests/tap-first.test.js`): opening Recipes draws the top bar, search, chips and the plates on the first screen at once and the other groups straight after; on the same slowed phone the first change shows in 0.13 to 0.38 s instead of 0.37 to 0.59 s, and every group is in after 0.41 to 0.76 s instead of 0.37 to 0.59 s.

## Installing it as an app

- The logo is a chalk "KB": off-white handwriting (Caveat) with an orange underline on a dark specials board, chosen by the head chef from five options (18 Oct 2026). The letters are drawn as paths, so no font is needed. `icons/` holds the app icons (512 with "kitchen book" and a flame; 192, 180 and 96 with just KB; the maskable one drawn smaller for Android's circle crop), `icon.svg` is the browser tab version, and the same mark sits in the top bar (`LOGO_MARK` in `js/app.js`) and on the QR sign. Below 720 px, the top bar uses the KB mark in place of the Kitchen Book title on every page, beside Back on task pages; the mark stays a Home link with a 44 px tap area. Larger screens keep their current branding. Phones that installed the app earlier may need it removed and added again to show the new icon (iPhone always does).
- The book is an installable web app: `manifest.webmanifest` (name, colours, icons in `icons/`, shortcuts to Prep list, Orders, Wastage and Recipes on a long press of the icon) and `sw.js` (service worker).
- Phones, tablets and computers that have not installed the book yet see an **Install the app** card at the top of the landing page (18 Oct 2026; computers from 20 Oct 2026). In Chrome, Edge and Samsung Internet it has an Install button; on iPhone and iPad it explains Share, then Add to Home Screen; on a Windows or Mac computer without the button it points to the install icon at the right of the address bar (in Edge, Apps, Install this site as an app), in Safari on a Mac it says File, Add to Dock, and in Firefox on a computer it says to use Edge or Chrome, because Firefox cannot install apps there; elsewhere it points to the browser menu. "Not now" hides it on that device only (`installNo` in the saved settings). It never shows in the installed app.
- Settings has the same **Install the app** section with the same wording for each device (`computerSteps()` in `js/app.js` is the computer text for both). Click tests: `tests/install-card.test.js` (Windows Chrome, Edge and Firefox, Mac Safari and Chrome, iPhone).
- The browser's install offer (`beforeinstallprompt`) is caught by a small script in `index.html` before the app scripts load, and `preventDefault()` is called on it, because Chrome only opens its install box from our button once that has been done. If the box fails to open, the card and Settings fall back to the manual steps. The manifest `id` is `/leichhardt-kitchen/`, so the app has its own identity on `kuschikuschbert.github.io` and cannot clash with another app on the same address.
- The service worker opens the book from the copy saved on the phone (21 Oct 2026; it was network first before), so the book opens at once even on weak kitchen wifi, and with no signal at all: the recipes work, and the kitchen pages say there is no signal until it is back (see Opened with no signal). Each opening checks for changes in the background. A complete new immutable generation is prepared before its active/previous pointers are published transactionally. For plain startup shell requests, a document with a proven binding uses its exact complete generation, including after a worker restart; a missing member is unavailable rather than replaced with an unrelated network file. Legacy or uncontrolled pages need a safe navigation to adopt a prepared generation. If something changed it tells the open book: in the first 8 seconds after opening, before anyone has touched the screen, the book reloads straight into the new version; later it shows the "New version available" bar and updates the same way as before (straight away when brought back, otherwise after 2 minutes untouched, or with Update now). The update and 04:00 refresh retain their guarded worker handshake; explicit retry retires a background round older than eight seconds, and an obsolete round cannot publish. An opening shares a round that is already running only before that round has asked for its files; after that it gets one more round once that one ends (4 Nov 2026). It used to share the round whatever its state, so a version that came out between two quick openings was not found: no `lk-new`, and the book waited for the strip. That was the cause of the flaky "just opened" checks in `tests/saved-copy.test.js`. See [saved-shell generations](docs/saved-shell-generations.md). The pages loaded only when they open (Manage recipes and the drawing editor, Manage functions, Delivery received, Supplier order) load their non-shell scripts with a version (`?v=N`): the worker keeps each under its plain address, one copy per file, network first past the browser's own copy (28 Oct 2026: GitHub Pages lets the browser keep a file for 10 minutes, so after an update a page opened again ran its old scripts, and the worker kept those for no signal), so once a page has opened with signal it opens with no signal too (28 Oct 2026; before, nothing with a `?` was kept, and after the book reloaded those pages said they could not open). Each page checks the version it got (`MG_API`, `FE_API`, `SUP_API` in `js/app.js` and the files' own `API`) and offers Reload the book for a different one. Those numbers stay as they are when a change only adds (the file checks for a new helper before it uses it, and the book checks for what the file added): a phone keeps each page's file for no signal, so a new number shut that page with no signal until it had opened once with signal, and Reload could not help (29 Oct 2026; the `?v=` address can still go up, so a browser with no worker in charge fetches the new file). `tests/saved-copy.test.js` opens Delivery received and the recipe and drawing editors with no signal from files kept before a change. Their helpers with no version (`js/library.js`, `js/lib/*.js`, `js/smart.js`, `js/compose.js`) are first fetched with the `X-LK-Fresh` header, which the worker sends to the network first, past the browser's own copy, and waits for the cache-write attempt before replying. A storage failure can leave the older lazy copy saved even when the network response succeeds. A start file (`SHELL`) asked for with a version is never kept under its plain address, since that would replace the saved shell one file at a time; nothing loads one that way. Staff phones still never fetch `js/manage.js`: it is not in `SHELL`. A new worker saves the shell past the browser's own cache (28 Oct 2026; GitHub Pages lets the browser keep a file for 10 minutes, so an install could save files from before a deploy next to new ones). The worker keeps the book's own files only: nothing from Firestore. Google Fonts and the Firebase files are kept once loaded (see Firebase kept on the phone). Firebase's requests for data (prep list, orders, wastage) are not touched. Since 26 Oct 2026 (#387) Firebase keeps that data, and changes not sent yet, in the tab's memory only (`docs/private-kitchen-access.md`), so with no signal the saved copy opens the book but not the kitchen's data. Startup files, the manifest and shell icons belong in `SHELL` and both workerless fallback lists. Other lazy assets retain their own cache behaviour; changing `CACHE` requires an explicit compatible migration, not a blanket cache reset. `tests/saved-copy.test.js` (its own copy of the test server, with every file held back 4 s, then a changed `js/data.js`; the five pages opened with no signal after a visit and a reload; a changed `js/smart.js` sent slowly; a staff phone with no `js/manage.js`; a new worker with the browser keeping files for 10 minutes, and a deploy within those 10 minutes for `js/smart.js` with `X-LK-Fresh` and `js/manage.js?v=4`, in the separate native HTTP-cache case described below; a start file asked for with a version).

The final saved-copy case uses a separate native HTTP-cache context with 600-second caching and no Playwright routes. Its entry and cached index serve the same marked inert HTML; an assertion checks the bare worker marker and absence of `LK_PREP`/`LK_FN` after controlled navigation. The earlier app cases retain their routed setup.
