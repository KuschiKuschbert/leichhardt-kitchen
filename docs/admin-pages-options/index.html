Admin pages: three designs

The Dashboard set a new standard. The other admin pages still look and behave like the older panel. Here is what drifts, the fixes every option shares, and three ways to organise the whole panel around the Dashboard's design.

Chosen 4 November 2026: B, Four rooms, on the shared page kit. Built in seven steps, one pull request each.

The brief

Your request, rewritten for a senior engineer.

The admin panel's information architecture and visual language are inconsistent. The new Dashboard (js/admin-dashboard.js, Pass board with written headlines) sets the bar: one toolbar, numbers first, trend chips, cards and charts with drill-through. The other 20 pages across the six chapters predate it and do not share its page anatomy, typography, control sizes, block styles or navigation depth.

Audit every admin page against the Dashboard. Propose three distinct, concrete directions for organising and styling the whole panel so it reads as one system. For each: mockups at phone (390 px) and laptop widths, the navigation model, where every existing page and control lands (no feature lost), the impact on reads, rules, roles and tests, the build in pull requests, and pros and cons.

No application changes until the head chef chooses. Roles, rules, read budgets, private drafts, Back and Resume, and save acknowledgements stay as they are in every option. All names and figures in the mockups are examples.

What drifts from the Dashboard today

Measured on 4 November 2026 by opening all 21 admin pages with the test stand-in at 1280 and 390 px.

  1. Up to four levels of navigation

    Chapter rail, then page pills, then On this page chips, then a view switch. Reports › Insights has 7 jump chips, Team has 9 over two lines, Wastage adds History, Summary and Daily reviews. The Dashboard has one level.

  2. Controls before content

    On Wastage, four rows of controls sit between the title and the first number: a 352 px period box, the view switch, a 156 px tall search box and More filters. On a phone the first entry starts about 1,130 px down. The Dashboard has one row: period and Refresh.

  3. Numbers last, in small type

    Wastage, Transfers and Team show totals as small inline text. Only Insights' Overview has big numbers. No page but the Dashboard uses its tiles, trend lines or Up 14, worse chips.

  4. Two surface styles, four heading styles

    The Dashboard uses white cards. Every other page uses flat ruled blocks, beige table heads and black segmented switches. Headings come numbered and orange (Insights), condensed with a kicker (Review desk), plain bold (the rest) and as card titles (Dashboard).

  5. Very long pages

    Team is 3,800 px on a laptop and 5,690 px on a phone, with 6 search boxes and 6 tables. Insights is 4,000 px. People is 2,480 px because every person is an open form: name box, role dropdown, four ticks, two links.

  6. Help text before the work

    Backup has eight lines of explanation before Download a backup. Order cut-offs has four before Add a supplier. Kitchen mail has three before the first message.

  7. Things said twice, or split up

    Dashboard tiles repeat Insights' Overview. Insights' By person repeats Team. The Training queue and saved Assessments sit in different chapters. Suppliers & pars is a page with two links.

  8. Controls in many sizes

    Period choice is a 352 px dropdown on Wastage, a small pill on Insights and pill buttons on the Dashboard. Search boxes run from 36 to 156 px tall. Role is a dropdown inside a table cell.

Shared fixes in every option

This page kit is step 1 whichever design you pick, so it can start safely before you decide the layout.

  • One page headerWhere you are, the title, one line of help, then one toolbar row: period pills like the Dashboard's, filters as pills, Refresh and Export at the right.
  • Numbers firstThe Dashboard's tiles where a page has numbers: the number, the change in words and colour, a small trend line. Only numbers the page already counts.
  • One block style per jobNumbers and charts in cards. Lists and tables ruled, with their own search in the block header. Settings as rows that say what they are set to.
  • One size for controlsPills choose, rectangles act, text boxes one height. No full-width dropdowns, no tall search boxes.
  • Two levels of navigation at mostChapter and page. On this page only on pages longer than two screens.
  • Help after the workOne line under the title. The rest opens from How this works.
  • The Dashboard's honest statesA failed read says Could not load, with Refresh. Never 0 or nobody. Waiting uses Ink in motion.

The three designs

Each design shows the same four jobs so you can compare like with like: a report (Wastage), the team (People), an inbox (Kitchen mail) and a setting (Order cut-offs). The laptop mockup is drawn at 1100 px and scaled to fit; scroll inside the phone.

A

The Pass board everywhere

Keep the six chapters and all 21 pages where they are. Rebuild each page with the Dashboard's anatomy: one toolbar, tiles with trends, then cards for charts, tables and settings. The smallest change to where things live, the biggest change to how they look.

  • Dashboard
  • Review desk Item links, Training, Proofreader
  • Reports Item evidence, Insights, Wastage, Transfers, Learning, Team
  • Saved records Links, Assessments, Guides
  • Operations Mail, Pings, Minutes, Suppliers, Cut-offs
  • Settings People, Backup and PIN
Show

Pros

  • Nothing moves. Every page, bookmark and habit stays where it is.
  • The Dashboard's look reaches every page, so the panel finally reads as one thing.
  • Lowest risk: page ids, tests' page helper, reads and rules are unchanged.
  • Ships one chapter at a time, each pull request useful on its own.

Cons

  • Does not fix the organisation: still 6 chapters and 21 pages, and the overlaps stay (Dashboard and Insights, Training apart from Assessments).
  • Brings cards back to every page, which reverses the 22 Oct 2026 decision "Fewer boxes on the admin panel".
  • Longest on a phone: tiles and card padding add height to already long pages.
  • Tiles on settings pages can feel forced; some pages have little worth counting.

B

Four rooms

Sort the panel by the job, not the data. Today is the Dashboard with Needs you grown into one To do list you can work through in place. Reports share one filter bar. Team gives each person one page. Kitchen is every setting as a row that says what it is set to, like the Settings page you chose on 3 Nov.

  • Today To do: mail, sign-offs, item links, training, proofreader, transfers. Then the Dashboard
  • Reports Prep, Wastage, Transfers, Orders, Item evidence, Learning
  • Team People with a page each, Assessments, Habits
  • Kitchen Sections, cut-offs, suppliers, pings, minutes, guides, records, access, backup, PIN
Show

Pros

  • Opens on what needs you, and each item is done without leaving Today.
  • Six chapters become four, with no page pills inside Today or Kitchen.
  • Pick a period once and every report keeps it.
  • "How is Sam going?" has one answer: access, activity and training on one page.
  • Reuses three patterns you already chose: the Dashboard, the Review desk's focused task and the Settings rows.

Cons

  • The most relearning. Pages you know move rooms, and the 1 Nov chapter list changes.
  • The person page brings activity and training together, so it must keep saying "Ticks record activity. They do not confirm skill."
  • More to build and test: a role walk-through for the person page and Today's in-place tasks.
  • Kitchen becomes a long list of rows on a phone, though each row is one line.

C

The workbench

Every page in one rail, with counts and a Find box, so any page is one tap away. Each page is a list on the left and the chosen thing on the right: a message, a person, a supplier, a wastage line. On a phone the list opens the detail full screen. The most app-like of the three.

  • Dashboard
  • To review Item links, Training, Proofreader
  • Reports Prep, Wastage, Transfers, Orders and team, Learning, Item evidence
  • Kitchen Mail, Pings, Minutes, Cut-offs, Suppliers
  • Records Links, Assessments, Guides
  • Settings People, Kitchen setup, Backup and PIN
Show

Pros

  • One level of navigation: every page and its count is visible at once.
  • Editing one thing at a time: no more open forms for every person or supplier.
  • Most on screen at once on a laptop. Fast to move between pages.
  • Scales: a new page is one more line in the rail.

Cons

  • The biggest rebuild: most pages' layout and many test selectors change.
  • Reverses the 1 Nov choice to keep page choices beside the title, not in a second sidebar level.
  • Suits mail, people and suppliers; long reads like Insights fit a list less well.
  • Laptop and phone differ more: the phone needs an Admin menu and full-screen details.
  • Counts in the rail need the Needs you numbers; until they load the rail shows none, never 0.

Recommendation

B, Four rooms, built on the shared page kit. It answers both halves of the request: the pages look like the Dashboard, and the panel is easier to find your way around. It reuses three patterns you already chose, so the new parts are familiar. It adds no reads and no rules.

If you want the least disruption, choose A: same places, new look. Choose C if you mostly work at a laptop and jump between many pages.

Mixing works too. A's tiles and cards fit B's reports. C's list and detail fits B's Team and Kitchen mail.

B was chosen on 4 November 2026. The build order (each its own pull request, with click tests at 390, 768 and 1280 px and the read budget check):

  1. Page kit: header, toolbar, tiles, cards, ruled lists, settings rows.
  2. Four rooms in the rail and the phone room switch, page ids unchanged.
  3. Today: To do with in-place tasks, the Dashboard under it.
  4. Reports: one filter bar and the same report layout.
  5. Team: person pages, with the role walk-through on the emulator.
  6. Kitchen: settings rows, records and access.
  7. Tidy up: remove the old chapter styles and update the notes.
All names, numbers, suppliers and messages in the mockups are examples, not the kitchen's records. No prices or supplier codes are shown or needed. Drafted 4 November 2026 for the head chef's choice.
