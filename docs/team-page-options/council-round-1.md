# Round 1: whether to build

10 November 2026, Queensland. Five fresh independent agents received the same proposal, current-contract packet, source `29258e6`, synthetic baseline screenshots and browser measurements. These are role-based reviews, not kitchen-staff research. The following are their actual returned reviews.

**Verdict: Build smaller, 5 of 5.** Explore the requested service and section controls in the existing notebook and recover space above the people. Cut a whole-app shell overhaul. No reviewer established staff frequency, labour savings or fewer errors. The new starter recommends separating membership from the section choice; the cook prioritises saved break returns remaining visible. These conditions must be reconciled in the previews.

## Cook mid-service

Verdict: Build smaller.

1. In two seconds I can spot break return times. I cannot scan the team: the direct phone shows one complete person, the chef panel none. Another picker alone does not fix that.
2. Section is already beside each name. A service picker could clarify whose service I am correcting, but two dropdowns plus Save ordinarily take five taps, against four today.
3. Save starts at 1367/1644 px. Wet-handed corrections need scrolling; large text and long labels already stretch one row to 284 px.

Best change: recover space above the people while keeping saved break returns visible, then fit the requested service and section controls together.

Contract fit: Needs work. Preserve 44 px targets, explicit membership changes, dated Save and independent drafts. A service-view change must never remove Dinner membership; an unsaved break edit must never alter the live board. Sage identifies people, not Saved.

Linear’s work-before-navigation principle helps. Reject its dense controls here.

Strongest evidence is the four rendered baselines and exercised task paths at source 29258e6. They establish crowding and taps with synthetic data. They do not establish Friday-service speed, fewer mistakes, contrast compliance or live persistence.

## Head chef

Verdict: Build smaller.

1. Head and sous chefs already correct another service in four taps, including Save. Two dropdowns would ordinarily make that five. We have no measured frequency or error rate to justify claiming labour savings.
2. The stronger measured problem is reaching the people. At 390 px, direct Team shows one complete row; the chef panel shows none. Doing nothing retains that scrolling burden.
3. C was just chosen and already supplies each person’s section picker. A whole-page overhaul adds development, checking and relearning costs before its extra value is established.

Best change: limit the next designs to service and section together beside each person within the existing worksheet, with a measured target for visible rows and correction taps. Cut the wider redesign.

Contract fit: retain sage people surfaces, paper interiors, the charcoal saved-break board, independent dated drafts and explicit dated Save. Changing the service being viewed must never remove membership or breaks. New-control contrast, recovery and error prevention remain Not evidenced.

Linear’s principle of making work more prominent than navigation is useful. Decorative cards would consume scarce space.

Evidence: inspected baseline images and synthetic measurements at source 29258e6. These establish layout and task paths, not staff demand or production reliability.

## Skeptic

Verdict: Build smaller.

1. C already provides inline sections, breaks, independent dated drafts and explicit Save. Another-service correction takes four taps today; two dropdowns would ordinarily make five. No measured correction frequency or staff research justifies rebuilding.
2. Visibility is the demonstrated weakness: one complete phone row directly, none in the chef panel. Risk / Not evidenced: a sous chef using large text cannot scan the person and controls together. Extra wrapping could increase wrong-row edits. Smallest check: compare 390 px rows and Save access.
3. Risk / Not evidenced: a chef switches Ann’s row from Lunch to Dinner, then changes her section. Expected: viewing Dinner preserves Lunch membership, sections and breaks. Mixed global and row contexts could target the wrong service. Smallest check: exercise both assignments, date navigation and dated Save.

Best change: add only the requested service view beside each existing section picker, with explicit service wording and no membership side effect.

Contract fit: preserve sage people surfaces, paper, the charcoal saved board and acknowledged Save. Screenshots and JSON at `29258e6` use synthetic data; new contrast, recovery and persistence remain unproved.

Linear’s work-before-navigation principle supports addressing measured overhead. It does not establish a case for rebuilding the page.

## New starter

Verdict: Build smaller.

1. A service control beside each name could help me understand whose Lunch or Dinner section I am changing. The section picker already exists. Calling both controls plainly matters more than refreshing the whole page.
2. The chef phone view initially shows no person; the direct view shows one. I must scroll past navigation and the saved break board before finding my task. Adding controls could increase that hunt.
3. “Not on Lunch” inside “Section” mixes working status with workplace. I could remove a service while trying to clear a section. A service dropdown also makes another-service corrections five taps rather than four. Long labels already become hard to read at largest text.

Best change: give each person one clearly labelled “Service to edit” and “Section” pair, with a separate, explicit action for changing whether they work that service.

Contract fit: the dated heading, written Saved status and separate saved break board help without colour. Preserve these and explicit dated Save. The fictional baseline demonstrates draft separation, not new-picker usability. Pending, failed reads, failed saves, contrast and recovery remain Not evidenced for the proposal.

Headspace’s short, consistent labels are useful. Reject decorative Airbnb cards that push people further down.

## Keeper of the book

Verdict: Build smaller.

Three reasons:

1. C already supplies sections. Cross-service correction costs four taps; two dropdowns likely cost five.
2. Chef screenshots show no initial person row, so added controls alone miss the larger obstruction.
3. Reusing the shared renderer, memory drafts and tokens avoids extra reads, shell files and broad testing.

Best change: define row service as date/person view state, separate from membership, then reuse existing Save and recovery.

Contract fit: baseline surfaces and saved/draft separation look consistent. JSON records 13 stand-in reads, three team/training. Server-rule costs, new contrast and enforcement remain Not evidenced. Flighty's principle supports reusing loaded information without listeners.

Break-it findings:

- **Risk:** date/global-service changes leave stale row context, altering the wrong assignment. Smallest check: two services across two dates, including off versus no section.
- **Checked in baseline fixture:** draft breaks leave the saved board untouched. **Risk:** held Save, repeat tap or lock falsely acknowledges or loses work. Extend existing held-write/recovery tests.
- **Risk:** stale authority or concurrent Save defeats protection, exposing private data or losing a plan. Source guards exist; mocks cannot prove enforcement. Reuse focused emulator role/version checks before readiness.

## Scope carried into round 2

Retain the app shell and notebook identity. Compare three credible arrangements inside the existing planner, all with the same required capabilities. Keep the saved break returns readable while making the selected people easier to reach. Name the cost of per-person service controls honestly. Prototype checks cannot establish live permissions, production persistence, lock/handover recovery or staff understanding.
