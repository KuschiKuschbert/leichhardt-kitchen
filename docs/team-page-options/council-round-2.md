## Cook mid-service

These differ structurally: A is one continuous worksheet, B separates planning into stages, C puts the roster first with a responsive workbench and reachable Save. The existing page already has row section pickers.

1. **C, best, 3 points.** At 19:30 with 40 dockets, seeing people earlier and saving without hunting matters. Take its prominent roster under Linear and nearby feedback under Duolingo. Weakness: the Save dock consumes reading space; changing date takes two actions instead of one. The laptop workbench keeps detailed editing beside people.
2. **A, second, 2 points.** Its visible week and familiar person disclosures make interrupted work easier to resume. Take its predictable arrangement under Headspace. Weakness: Save starts at 1570 px on the phone, below eight people. Wet hands make that scroll consequential even though tap counts exclude it.
3. **B, third, 1 point.** Full-day Review brings assignments together, useful under Airbnb. Take that review contribution, but reject mandatory stages under Todoist: a section correction takes four actions versus three in A/C, and remembering the stage interrupts service.

An A+B+C blend has no demonstrated advantage over C. A’s permanent week displaces people; B’s stages conflict with C’s direct editing and Save. Keep useful whole-day review within the existing disclosure.

**Contract fit: Works in the synthetic preview** for surface meanings, explicit draft/saved separation and membership handling. The frozen evidence passes 228 checks, including C’s repaired keyboard obstruction. Phone previews show two complete rows in A/C versus one in B; none fits a complete person at largest text. These are Preview figures against baseline `29258e6`, not delivered density. Real persistence, recovery, wet-hand speed, midnight and 04:00 remain unproved; complete-app checks are required.

Recommend C. Its main downside is the dock’s space cost at large text.

## Reconciliation and later repair

The head chef and Skeptic prefer A for the smallest change and permanently visible week. The cook, new starter, Keeper and designer prefer C for reachable dated Save, earlier people and focused details. This is a real disagreement, not unanimous agreement. All six reject compulsory B stages and see no demonstrated advantage from an all-three blend.

During the original review, designer and Keeper independently reproduced B's local text overflow. The Assignments button ended at x135.656 px; its text ended at x154.984 px, 19.328 px beyond the button and 11.328 px into the next control's start. Page containment and parent-background contrast sampling did not detect that local paint defect. It was not a data or permission failure. The original source, JSON and four stress viewport screenshots are archived separately. The lead's later repair stacks B's stage controls at largest phone text, preserving font size. New measurements and focused reviewer follow-up follow; original ranks above remain original ranks.
