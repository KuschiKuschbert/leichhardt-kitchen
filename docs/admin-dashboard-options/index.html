Admin panel / Dashboard proposals / 3 November 2026

Two ways to see the kitchen at a glance

The admin panel already holds a lot: 60 days of prep, wastage, transfers, training, mail and the nightly analysis. Today you dig through Reports one page at a time to find it. These ideas put the important numbers on one page, say whether they are getting better, and take you straight to the detail.

Idea 1

A dashboard opens first

A new first chapter, Dashboard, before Review desk. The existing reports stay as the detail behind every number.

Idea 2

Needs you

One list of everything waiting on you: open mail, minutes not signed, item links, training gaps, loans not back and any nightly job that failed. Each row opens the exact place.

Idea 3

Compared with what?

Every number shows the period before it, in words as well as colour. More lines ticked is better. More waste is worse.

Idea 4

Tap a number, see where it came from

Every tile, bar and cell opens the existing report with the same dates and section already set.

Idea 5

The week ahead on one screen

Functions and guest numbers, supplier cut-offs and the days that are usually busiest, side by side for rostering and ordering.

Idea 6

A watch list

Pin an item, a section or a person from any report. It stays on the dashboard with its trend until you unpin it.

Idea 7

Written headlines

Plain sentences written by fixed rules from the numbers: "Waste is up on last week, mostly Overproduction in Larder." No AI service, the same rules every night.

Idea 8

Opens fast and cheap

The nightly job already reads 90 days. It would also write one small dashboard record, so the page opens with a handful of reads instead of reading 60 days of lines on the tablet.

Design
Screen

A. The Pass board

Everything at once, like the board on the pass. Six numbers with their trend, what needs you, the week ahead, then the charts. Change the period once and the whole page follows.

B. The Morning brief

A short written brief, read top to bottom in two minutes before service. What to do first, what changed and why, and what is coming. The full numbers sit at the side, or at the end on a phone.

Recommendation

Build A, and put B's three headlines at its top

A fits the Chef's Desk you chose on 1 November and keeps the most information one tap away. B's written headlines answer "what changed?" faster than any chart, so they would sit as one strip above the tiles. If you mostly check on your phone before service, choose B on its own instead.

What stays the same

Who sees it. Only the verified head chef admin, like the reports now. A cut-down version for sous chefs would be a separate decision.

No prices. No costs, GP or supplier codes from AVC Live. Waste is counted in lines and amounts, never dollars.

No made-up limits. Delivery temperatures show what was recorded. The dashboard does not mark a reading as passed or failed.

No guessing. A number that did not load says so. It never shows 0 or "nobody" in its place.

The reports stay. Insights, Wastage, Transfers, Team and Item evidence keep their pages. The dashboard links into them.

The look. Barlow and Barlow Condensed, warm paper, the charcoal bar and Leichhardt orange, as on the Chef's Desk.

If you choose one

  1. Nightly dashboard record. The nightly analysis job writes one small private record with the numbers for 7, 30 and 90 days. Admin only in the rules.
  2. Dashboard page. The new first chapter, the tiles or the brief, linked into the existing reports.
  3. Needs you. Counts from what the admin panel already reads, each opening its exact page.
  4. Week ahead and watch list. Functions and cut-offs already loaded, then pins saved with the admin's records.
