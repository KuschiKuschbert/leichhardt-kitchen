## Food safety source verification

**Verified:** [FSANZ cooling and reheating guidance](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/food-safety/cooling-and-reheating-food) states cooked potentially hazardous food must cool from 60°C to 21°C within two hours, then from 21°C to 5°C within four hours. Another process requires evidence that it keeps food safe. The guidance also discusses checking cooling progress with a probe and substantiating food-safety controls. This verifies the requested two-stage targets; it does not validate a venue-specific SOP.

**Verified:** [FSANZ 2-hour/4-hour rule](https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/food-safety/2-hour-4-hour-rule) concerns food outside refrigeration between 5°C and 60°C and cumulative exposure through preparation, storage, transport and display. It must not be used as the batch-cooling workflow or confused with the requested presets.

**Unknown:** The venue’s approved cooling SOP, approved exceptions, thermometer/calibration practice, accepted process start evidence and corrective actions were not supplied to this research agent. Do not invent them. The design should visibly require the venue SOP before configuring recorded cooling outcomes. A timer expiring means a check/action is needed, never that cooling succeeded.

**Proposed:** Store measured-at separately from entered-at; stage deadlines derive from the actual process/transition time. A delayed entry never adds cooling time. A linked four-hour stage must reference its same batch and first-stage measurement. Standalone four-hour reminders remain reminders. Keep original evidence, late/missing readings and corrections in the timeline; acknowledgement alone records no temperature and completes no cooling record.

## Browser and notification evidence

**Verified:** [MDN setTimeout](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/setTimeout) explains background throttling and late execution. Chrome can check timers only once per minute under intensive throttling; Android Firefox may unload inactive pages. A countdown is therefore a view of an absolute deadline, not a trustworthy background scheduler.

**Verified:** [MDN Notifications usage](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Notifications_API/Using_the_Notifications_API) requires permission, recommends requesting it only from a user action and notes secure-context requirements. Permission can remain default, be granted or be denied. Desktop Notification construction is not portable to mobile; a service worker’s showNotification is the appropriate mobile mechanism. This displays a notification when code runs, not a guaranteed future alarm.

**Verified:** [MDN Push API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Push_API) describes server-delivered messages when the app is not loaded, requiring an active service worker and push subscription. [WebKit iOS/iPadOS Web Push](https://webkit.org/blog/13878/web-push-for-web-apps-on-ios-and-ipados/) documents Home Screen web-app support from 16.4, permission from a direct user interaction, lock-screen delivery and Focus controls. Actual support must still be tested on the venue’s current devices.

| Situation | Proposed first-release promise | Verification after implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visible page, enabled sound | Absolute due state and best-effort audible alert after user enables/tests sound. Never call it a guaranteed safety alarm. | Controlled clock; enabled/muted audio; multiple batches and tabs; duplicate suppression. |
| Background tab | Deadline remains authoritative; callbacks/notification may be delayed. Reconcile on visibility/focus. | Real supported browsers; background longer than throttling threshold. |
| Locked/suspended device | Local page timers may stop; do not promise an alarm. In-app overdue state appears when resumed. | Physical Android/iPhone/shared tablet with lock, Focus and battery controls. |
| Closed browser/app | No local countdown-based alarm promise. | Close and reopen; deadlines unchanged, missed check visible. |
| Permission denied/unsupported | Persistent in-app due state and clear “notifications off/unavailable” text. | Denial, unsupported API and granted permission. |
| Poor connectivity | Saved deadline persists; pending observations remain unconfirmed and carry original measured/entered times. | Offline, retry, second device, handover and failed save. |

**Proposed optional additional infrastructure:** Closed-app push needs a server-side deadline scheduler, permission-aware subscriptions, service-worker push handler, retry/idempotent delivery IDs and health monitoring. Reuse the existing notification stack only after its actual capabilities and timing are verified. A ten-minute scheduled job is not a precise cooling alarm. Push still depends on connectivity, OS permissions, Focus and delivery services; it cannot certify that a person saw the alert. Physical-device tests remain required. First-release recommendation is honest in-app status plus supported enabled audio/notification, with closed-app push explicitly scoped as extra work if the venue needs it.
