# Round 2: three rendered concepts

Six fresh agents independently received the same neutral packet, all three phone concepts, actual-app baseline, B's laptop layout and busy/overdue examples. Each used its role brief and scored first place 3, second 2, third 1. No preferred design was supplied. This is simulated role-based review, not research with real staff or an accessibility certification.

## Recommendation

**C, with B's written View cue and explicit supplier actions inside.** It retains five complete phone requests instead of two in the baseline and a bounded 69 px closed deadline treatment. Confirming a supplier adds one disclosure tap. The requests and deadlines stay in one place without a jump or a second purchasing workflow.

Use **Add request** across the concepts to answer the new starter's wording concern. Keep one tap to open the same Add form and one item tick meaning actually ordered. Other reviewers' preference for a familiar entry is retained through position and behaviour; only its label is clarified.

Do not add B's desktop column in the first build by default. It was suggested by the screen designer, but adds layout scope and was not the common recommendation. The owner can choose that mix explicitly.

## Disagreement and limits

The new starter ranked B first because View is explicit; the other five preferred C's in-place interaction. There is a real cost to the recommendation: every supplier confirmation needs two taps when C is closed, and other suppliers' names and delivery dates are hidden until it opens. Its total due and overdue count must remain visible and correct. All reviewers rejected A for busy-day request visibility, despite its direct actions.

No reviewer established a production time saving, fewer missed orders, real-device readability, accessible behaviour in all configurations, live permissions, acknowledgement or offline recovery. Those remain implementation checks. Small recipe and attribution text follows the existing book but still needs a service-device check.
